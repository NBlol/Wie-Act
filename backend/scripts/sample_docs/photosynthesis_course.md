# Photosynthesis — course notes (synthetic teaching material)

## 1. Overview

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. The overall equation is 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light → C6H12O6 + 6 O2. Photosynthesis takes place mainly in the leaves, inside organelles called chloroplasts.

## 2. Chlorophyll and light absorption

Chlorophyll is the green pigment located in the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast. Chlorophyll absorbs light energy mostly in the blue (around 430 nm) and red (around 660 nm) wavelengths and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green. Accessory pigments such as carotenoids widen the range of absorbed wavelengths and protect the plant from excess light.

## 3. Light-dependent reactions

The light-dependent reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes. Light energy excites electrons in chlorophyll; water molecules are split (photolysis), releasing oxygen as a by-product. The energy is used to produce ATP and NADPH, two energy carriers that power the next stage.

## 4. The Calvin cycle (light-independent reactions)

The Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma of the chloroplast. The enzyme RuBisCO fixes carbon dioxide onto a five-carbon sugar (RuBP). Using ATP and NADPH from the light-dependent reactions, the cycle produces three-carbon sugars (G3P) that are assembled into glucose. The Calvin cycle does not need light directly, but it depends on the products of the light reactions.

## 5. Factors affecting the rate of photosynthesis

Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature are the main limiting factors. Increasing light intensity raises the rate until another factor becomes limiting. Very high temperatures denature enzymes such as RuBisCO and the rate falls sharply. Stomata regulate gas exchange: they open to let CO2 in and close to limit water loss.

## 6. Photosynthesis versus cellular respiration

Cellular respiration is essentially the reverse process: glucose and oxygen are consumed to release energy (ATP), producing carbon dioxide and water. Respiration happens in the mitochondria of all living cells, day and night, while photosynthesis happens only in chloroplasts in the presence of light. Plants perform both processes.

## 7. Common misconceptions

- Plants do NOT get their food from the soil; they build glucose from CO2 and water.
- Photosynthesis consumes CO2 and releases O2 — it does not produce CO2.
- Plants respire too: at night they release CO2 like animals.
- Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light; it does not absorb green light.
